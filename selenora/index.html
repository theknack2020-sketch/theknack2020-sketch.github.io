Selenora: Where the Moon Rises

Tonight's moon, from where you stand: when it rises, where on the horizon, how much is lit, and whether tonight is a full, new or supermoon. Everything is computed on your device from the date and a place — no account, no network, no ads, no astrology.

One object does every job

The disc is the moon tonight, drawn with the real terminator. Its rim is your horizon with north at the top. A tick crossing the rim marks a rise or a set at its bearing, with the time outside; the long ochre tick is what happens next. A dot on the rim is where the moon is right now — filled when it is up, hollow when it is under.

Tonight

Moonrise and moonset with the time and the compass bearing, phase name and illumination, distance and the supermoon flag, the next full and new moon, and one plain sentence for the night: Rises 16:44 in the northeast, 55 min before sunset. 99% lit. Full at 04:28 tonight, a supermoon.

Calendar and events

A month grid with the terminator drawn for every night and the moonrise time per day; every full and new moon of the year with local times, blue moons flagged; supermoons and lunar eclipses computed years ahead, each with whether you will see it from your place.

Places

Use the device's location once, or pick a city from the list built into the app. Add a place by coordinates. One saved place is free.

Widgets, Lock Screen, StandBy, Apple Watch

A small Home Screen widget with tonight's disc and the next rise or set is always included. Selenora Pro adds the medium and large widgets, Lock Screen and StandBy accessories, and Apple Watch complications with a glance app on the wrist.

What is free, what is Pro

Free forever: Tonight, the calendar, one saved place, one small Home Screen widget, the full-moon notification and events for the next 90 nights. Pro (one monthly plan, one yearly plan, 7-day free trial): every widget size and surface, Apple Watch complications, unlimited places, moonrise, new-moon, supermoon and eclipse alerts, and events beyond 90 nights.

Accuracy

Rise, set and phase times are computed to the minute with published astronomical algorithms (Meeus) and checked against the U.S. Naval Observatory's tables and NASA's eclipse catalogue.
