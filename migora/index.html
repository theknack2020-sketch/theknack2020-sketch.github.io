Migora: Migraine Tracker

Version 2.0 from TheKnack

Migora: Migraine Tracker is a private migraine and headache diary for iPhone and iPad: you log an attack in a few taps on a true-black screen, and Migora records the air pressure around your attacks. There is no account, no ads and no Migora server, and Migora never uploads your diary. Migora is a tracking tool, not a medical device. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.

Who it is for

  • People with migraine or frequent headaches who want to log an attack fast, in a dark room, with no typing.
  • People who want to see the air pressure around their attacks, from their own device and Apple Weather.
  • People who want a record for an appointment: a one-page summary of the last 30 days is included, and the full doctor report comes with Migora Pro.
  • People who want a diary with no account and no server.

What it is not

  • Migora is a tracking tool, not a medical device. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Always consult a healthcare professional.
  • It does not predict migraines: it describes the pressure and your own log.
  • It is not a synced diary: there is no iCloud sync and no account, so the log stays on the device where you made it.
  • It does not connect to Apple Health.

What it does

Attack Mode

  • Attack Mode opens on a true-black screen with large targets and no typing: pick the pain from 1 to 10, tap where it hurts, mark aura, and tap Start Logging.
  • While Attack Mode is open, the screen is dimmed to 20%; a switch in Settings turns this off.
  • During the attack you can update the pain, tap “I took medication” for a dose, and end it with “Feeling Better”.
  • Afterward, the recap adds symptoms, suspected triggers, what you tried, doses with “Add a Dose” and whether each one helped, and a note.
  • On iPhone, a running attack shows as a Live Activity on the Lock Screen, with “I Took Medication” and “Feeling Better” buttons.
  • Saying “Log a migraine in Migora” to Siri records the start of an attack without opening the app, and on iOS 18 or later a Log Attack control opens Attack Mode.

Pressure

  • Migora reads the barometer in your iPhone or iPad, when it has one, while the app is open, and adds hourly pressure from Apple Weather for your approximate area.
  • Today’s pressure and the past 48 hours, with your attacks marked on the curve, are included.
  • With Migora Pro, the chart adds the next 48 hours from Apple Weather, and a pressure band (Steady, Shifting or Rapid swings) describes the last 6 hours and the next 24.
  • Pressure reads in hPa, inHg or mmHg, set for your region unless you choose one in Settings.
  • A widget for the Home Screen and the Lock Screen shows the current pressure; the band is part of Migora Pro.
  • Siri answers “How’s the pressure today in Migora” with the pressure and which way it is moving; with Migora Pro it adds the band.

Diary and history

  • Attack logging is unlimited, and your full history and calendar are never locked.
  • You can add an earlier attack, correct its time, pain or aura later, and undo a deletion.
  • A daily check-in notes sleep quality, stress, hydration, mood and cycle phase, with an optional reminder that skips days you have already checked in.
  • Your triggers start from a ready-made list that you can rename, merge or delete.
  • Your medications are your own list: Migora never suggests medications or doses, and a medication you stop keeps its past doses.

For your doctor

  • Included: a one-page PDF of your last 30 days with attack days, attacks, peak pain range, acute-medication days and your latest days-affected count.
  • With Migora Pro: the full report for the last 30, 90, 180 or 365 days, with a calendar heat map, attack durations, acute-medication days by month, your most-tagged triggers and your days-affected trend.
  • Both can carry your name, kept only on this device, and open in Share or Print.
  • Every few months, a three-month check-in counts the days migraines stopped you or slowed you to half pace, and adds the headache days from your log; the trend comes with Migora Pro.

Insights

  • With Migora Pro, Insights lays your attacks over 30 or 90 days of pressure and shows how often each trigger was tagged and how your medications rated by your own marks.
  • Everyone sees this month’s acute-medication days.

How it works

  • Two pressure sources: the barometer in your device, read only while Migora is open, and Apple Weather’s hourly pressure for your approximate area, the past 24 hours and the next 48 at each refresh.
  • Barometer readings are split at every fast change in height, such as an elevator ride, and a drop is only looked for inside one consistent series, so moving up or down is not read as weather.
  • A rapid drop is a fall of at least 3 hPa within 3 hours: a fixed threshold, not a personal one.
  • An attack counts on every calendar day it touched; an attack Migora closed after 72 hours counts on its first day only until you set or keep its end.
  • Average durations leave out attacks whose end Migora estimated.

Privacy

No account, no ads, no analytics and no Migora server: the app has no networking code of its own. Only if you allow location, a reduced-accuracy location goes to Apple Weather; Migora keeps it on the device with your readings, rounded to a tenth of a degree. There is no iCloud sync; Migora does not exclude its data from the device backups you make yourself. App Lock asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, and Delete All Data erases the log with no copy kept.

Questions

Is there a migraine diary app that shows the air pressure around attacks?

Yes. Migora marks your attacks on the pressure curve, from your device’s barometer and Apple Weather. Today’s pressure and the past 48 hours are included; the next 48 hours and the pressure band come with Migora Pro.

Does Migora alert me when the pressure drops?

Yes, with Migora Pro, if you turn on Pressure-Drop Alerts and allow notifications and location. You get at most one alert a day, when Apple Weather’s forecast near you shows a fall of at least 3 hPa within 3 hours, and the alert says when the fall is expected. Since 2.0 your logged attacks no longer keep the alert quiet, and iOS decides when the background check runs, so the timing is not exact.

Can I log a migraine attack quickly, in the dark?

Yes. Attack Mode is true black with large targets and no typing: pain, where it hurts and aura, then Start Logging. Medication, symptoms, triggers and notes can wait for the recap.

Can I get a headache report for my doctor?

Yes. A one-page PDF of your last 30 days is included. Migora Pro adds the full report, up to 12 months, with a calendar heat map; it is a record of what you logged.

Can I see how many migraine days I had each month?

The calendar marks every day with a logged attack, month by month, but shows no total. The included one-page summary counts the attack days in the last 30 days, and Migora Pro’s full report counts them over 30 days to 12 months with a heat map for each month.

Can I export my migraine log as CSV?

Yes, at any time and without Migora Pro: Settings, Export All Data as CSV. You get files for attacks, the pain timeline, medication doses, medications, check-ins and the three-month check-in, plus a README; pressure history is optional.

Is my migraine log uploaded anywhere?

No. Migora has no server and no account, and it never uploads your diary; only an approximate location goes to Apple Weather, if you allow it. There is no iCloud sync, but your own iCloud or computer backup of the device can include Migora’s data.

Is Migora a medical device?

No. Migora is a tracking tool, not a medical device. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Always consult a healthcare professional.
