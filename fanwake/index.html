FanWake: Flash & Light Alarm

Version 3.1.0 · November 12, 2026 · from TheKnack

FanWake: Flash & Light Alarm is an alarm clock for iPhone and iPad that you can see: open it when it rings, or leave it open on its bedside clock, and the screen flashes between two colors you pick, the flashlight can blink with it, and the phone buzzes on every change. It is made for people who want to see and feel an alarm as well as hear it, such as heavy sleepers and people who are hard of hearing. The flash runs while FanWake is open, so for a light that starts on its own, leave FanWake open on its bedside clock, on the charger.

Who it is for

  • Heavy sleepers who want light, vibration and a wake-up challenge on top of sound.
  • People who are hard of hearing and can keep the phone where they will see it, ideally open on the bedside clock.
  • People who want a dim bedside clock and sleep sounds next to their alarm.

What it is not

  • It is not a dedicated alerting device for deaf people: with the phone locked, the screen flash and flashlight start once you open FanWake, unless you left it open on the bedside clock.
  • On iOS 17 to 25 with the ringer on silent, the alarm notification only vibrates.
  • The blinking flashlight needs a camera flash: every iPhone has one, most iPads do not.

What it does

How it rings

  • On iOS 26 and later, once you allow alarms, FanWake rings as a system alarm on the Lock Screen, even on silent and in Focus; open it while it rings and the color flash starts, with your challenge if you set one.
  • On iOS 17 to 25, with notifications allowed, each alarm is a time-sensitive notification that repeats every 30 seconds for about five minutes, even in Focus, and tapping it starts the flash. FanWake can plan these repeats only for the soonest alarm times, counted from the last time FanWake ran (opened, or Stop, Snooze or a tap on its notification): about the next six alarm times when you have one to three alarms (six mornings with one daily alarm, two mornings with three), fewer with alarms on chosen days, with many alarms, or while an alarm is snoozed or ringing. After that an alarm may ring only once, and with very many alarms some may not ring at all, in which case FanWake warns you on its home screen. Each time FanWake runs, it plans the repeats again.
  • Left open on its bedside clock, on the charger, FanWake takes the screen with the flash at alarm time, with sound even on silent.
  • Snooze rings again even when FanWake is closed, and alarms keep their time when you travel.

When the flash starts

  • Your two colors take turns on the screen, between a solid bar at the top with the time and one at the bottom with Stop and Snooze.
  • Slow and Medium speeds are included; Fast and Accelerating come with FanWake Pro.
  • The flashlight blinks with every change, switched on or off for each alarm, on an iPhone or an iPad that has a camera flash.
  • The phone buzzes on every change, and the sound builds over 30 seconds: Classic, Digital, Gentle or Random.

Prove you’re awake

  • Math (add or multiply two numbers) and Shake (shake the phone 15 times) are included; Typing (type a short phrase) and Memory (match 6 pairs of colors) come with FanWake Pro, or you can use no challenge at all.
  • On iOS 26 the Lock Screen always shows Stop; on an alarm with a challenge, Stop brings the alarm back two minutes later, up to 10 times, until you finish the challenge in FanWake.

Colors

  • 24 color pairs and 26 ready-made themes, or any two colors you like with FanWake Pro.
  • Make and edit your own themes: 5 saved without FanWake Pro, unlimited with it.
  • Your next alarm, in its own colors, is the home screen, and Preview shows its flash.

Bedside clock and sleep sounds

  • A large, dim clock with your next alarm under it, opened from Settings or by saying “Start bedside in FanWake”.
  • 5 sleep sounds with a 15 to 90 minute timer, which stop when an alarm rings, and a bedtime timer that sets the alarm for how long you want to sleep.

Also

  • A wake-up streak, a streak freeze for one missed morning, and goals at 7, 14, 30, 60 and 100 days.
  • Siri and Shortcuts can set an alarm, tell you the next one, and turn one on or off.

How it works

  • FanWake changes color at most twice a second, and asks about flashing light the first time you open it.
  • With Reduce Flash in FanWake’s settings, the colors fade once every 3 seconds and the flashlight stays steady; the same happens when Reduce Motion is on in the system’s accessibility settings.
  • How the alarm reaches you depends on the iOS version: on iOS 26 and later, once you allow alarms, FanWake sets system alarms; otherwise, and on iOS 17 to 25, it uses a repeating time-sensitive notification if notifications are allowed, planned for your soonest alarms; with neither allowed, it rings only while FanWake is open on screen.
  • FanWake uses the 12- or 24-hour clock your iPhone is set to.

Privacy

No account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your alarms stay on your device. The App Store privacy label says Data Not Collected.

Questions

Is there an iPhone alarm that flashes the screen in colors?

Yes. When a FanWake alarm goes off and FanWake is open, the screen flashes between two colors you pick, at Slow or Medium speed, or Fast and Accelerating with FanWake Pro. Left open on its bedside clock, FanWake starts the flash by itself at alarm time.

Is there an alarm app that blinks the flashlight?

Yes. FanWake can blink the flashlight with every color change while the alarm flashes, on an iPhone or an iPad with a camera flash; you switch it on or off for each alarm.

Can FanWake help if I am deaf or hard of hearing?

It adds light and vibration to an alarm, with one main limit: the screen flash and the flashlight run while FanWake is open. For a light that starts by itself, leave FanWake open on its bedside clock, on the charger; otherwise the alarm rings or vibrates and the flash starts when you open the app.

Does it ring when my iPhone is on silent?

On iOS 26 and later, yes: once you allow alarms, it rings on the Lock Screen even on silent and in Focus. On iOS 17 to 25, with notifications allowed, the alarm is a repeating notification, and on silent the iPhone only vibrates. FanWake can plan these repeats only for the soonest alarm times, counted from the last time FanWake ran (opened, or Stop, Snooze or a tap on its notification): about the next six alarm times when you have one to three alarms (six mornings with one daily alarm, two mornings with three), fewer with alarms on chosen days, with many alarms, or while an alarm is snoozed or ringing. After that an alarm may ring only once (on silent, a single vibration), and with very many alarms some may not ring at all, in which case FanWake warns you on its home screen. Each time FanWake runs, it plans the repeats again. Left open on the bedside clock, FanWake plays sound even on silent.

Does it have wake-up challenges for heavy sleepers?

Yes. Math and Shake are included; Typing and Memory come with FanWake Pro. On iOS 26, on an alarm with a challenge, Stop brings the alarm back two minutes later, up to 10 times, until you finish the challenge.

Can I use it as a bedside clock?

Yes. The bedside clock is a large, dim clock with your next alarm under it, and 5 sleep sounds play on a 15 to 90 minute timer that stops when an alarm rings.

How fast does it flash, and can I soften it?

At most twice a second. FanWake asks about flashing light the first time you open it, and with Reduce Flash the colors fade once every 3 seconds and the flashlight stays steady.

Do I need FanWake Pro?

No. 3 alarms, 24 color pairs and 26 themes, Slow and Medium flash, the Math and Shake challenges, the bedside clock and sleep sounds work without FanWake Pro. If FanWake Pro ends, every alarm you set keeps ringing as you set it.
