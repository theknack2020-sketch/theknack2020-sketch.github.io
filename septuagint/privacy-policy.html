Privacy Policy

Septuagint, version 2 · Last updated 10 November 2026

The short version

The App collects nothing and sends nothing. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. Nothing you read, search for or write leaves your device.

Data we collect

None. We operate no server and receive no information about you or your use of the App. Its App Store privacy label is "Data Not Collected".

Data stored on your device

Your bookmarks, highlights, notes, reading position and reading-plan progress are stored on your device by the App. They are not transmitted anywhere and we have no access to them. They are part of your own device backup if you keep one. Deleting the App deletes them.

So that you can find a book, a chapter or a psalm from your device's own search, the App adds their titles and opening words to the search index your device keeps. That index stays on the device, and nothing you bookmarked, highlighted or wrote is in it. Shortcuts you set up for the App run on your device.

Connections

The App's own code makes no network connection. Three things you may choose to do are carried out by Apple's software and reach the internet as they would from any app:

  • Buying or restoring a subscription, which is handled by Apple.
  • Opening this policy, the Terms of Use, the Support page, a licence or a source's website, which are web pages shown by Safari.
  • Opening an App Store page, to rate the App or to look at another app of ours.

Permissions

The App requests no permissions at all: no camera, no photo library, no notifications, no location, no contacts, no microphone.

Purchases

Subscriptions are handled entirely by Apple. We never see your payment details. Apple may provide us with anonymous, aggregated sales statistics that cannot identify you.

Children

The App is suitable for general audiences and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the revised version will appear on this page with a new date. Since we collect nothing, any change is likely to concern wording, not practice.
